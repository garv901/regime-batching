## Closest threats to novelty

1. **PackInfer (2602.06072) + PersistentKV (2606.26666)**: both say heterogeneous-length batches cause stragglers/under-utilization and fix it at the kernel/work-assignment level, PersistentKV with a cost-model per-step dispatch. Differences: both are decode-only/kernel-only; no hybrid (prefill chunk + decode) steps, no vLLM CUDA-graph boundary, no scheduler co-design, no length oracle. Reviewer will ask "why not just use a load-balanced kernel (FlashInfer/PackInfer)?" -- we need to show FA3+vLLM default is what people run and that scheduler co-design adds beyond the kernel.
2. **CascadeInfer (2512.19179)**: explicitly argues schedulers ignore the attention backend's sensitivity to length heterogeneity, implemented on vLLM. Differences: multi-instance (16 GPU) length-grouping, our setting is one GPU/one engine; it avoids mixing regimes (like disaggregation) while we make mixing cheap via kernel dispatch plus oracle planning. Our novelty statement should be scoped "single-engine".
3. **vLLM PR #58013 / forks (#848, vllm-metal #851) and Past-Future (2507.10150)**: #58013 and the forks show maintainers/third parties already fix "decode rows in mixed varlen call lose split-KV" by splitting the call (FA2/Metal). Past-Future does peak-memory admission. Our novelty cannot claim "first to notice decodes in mixed calls under-split"; it must claim the FA3 + CUDA-graph-boundary mechanism (max_num_splits=32 only in full graph; heuristic=1 otherwise), the cost model, and oracle upper bound. Also a competing fix to benchmark: two-call split.

I found no paper doing scheduler x attention-kernel-dispatch co-design within one engine with (oracle or predicted) output-length foresight. Absence-of-evidence only: searches were limited to arXiv API/WebSearch, not exhaustive over OSDI/SOSP/MLSys/EuroSys proceedings.
