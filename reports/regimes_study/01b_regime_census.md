# Stage 1b. Regime census: every kind of step, its whole-step cost, and the best reachable alternative

Status: done (in-engine job 1452, kernel-level job 1453, 2026-10-07). vLLM 0.30.0, FA3, one H100-80GB, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct.

## Question

Stage 1 measured only attention, and went deep on two regimes (X and long-prefix appends). For every kind of step
vLLM forms (decode-only of any size and context mix, prefill alone, mixed, long-prefix), where does the step's
time go (GEMMs and other non-attention kernels, attention, CPU launch), how far is it from the best reachable cost
for the same work, and what reaches it (kernel configuration or batch formation)?

## Prediction (written before the run)

Quantitative: `analysis/census_predict.py` predicts every census step and every F7 attention cell. Whole step =
tile-model attention (32 layers) + non-attention cost by graph mode, fit on the earlier Llama-3.1-8B telemetry
(job 1040; 6,000 steps; mean error 2.9% full-graph decode, 4.1% piecewise, 4.5% eager):

| Graph mode | When | Predicted step time |
|---|---|---|
| FULL | decode-only, T <= capture size | attention + max(4.21 ms, 0.020 ms x T) + 1.92 ms |
| PIECEWISE | prefill in the step, T <= capture size | attention + max(4.21 ms, 0.020 ms x T) + 2.37 ms |
| NONE (eager) | T > capture size | max(16.8 ms, attention + max(4.21 ms, 0.020 ms x T) + 2.48 ms) |

Written to `results/regimes_study/census_pred_pre.jsonl` before either job produced census data: first at
06:59 UTC (sha256 `2df85b74...afa`, commit 842a089), then regenerated at 08:12 UTC (sha256 `afa72c14...7048`)
after the grid moved from 128k to 120k contexts (see Method). The fit is unchanged and all 2,061 predictions
for shapes in both grids are identical; only the 120k shapes are new. The 16.8 ms eager floor is the CPU launch time of one step; it was
measured when the node's load average was about 650 and is the least certain constant.

Qualitative:
1. Decode-only steps sit at the weight or KV bandwidth floor; the attention kernel moves them by <= 5%. Their only
   lever is batch size (cost per token).
2. Steps above the capture size pay the eager launch cost, which capture 2048 removes up to 2,048 tokens.
3. X steps lose to both the split choice and the eager cliff; capture 2048 fixes both, because FA3 then gets split
   bound 32.
4. Long-prefix appends keep the H5 loss under every engine config. A small remainder (<= 64 tokens on 120k) in a
   step of <= 512 tokens costs <= 0.25 ms of attention per layer.

Decode sets: none; 1 x 120k; 3 x 120k; 4 x 24k; 31 x 1k + 1 x 120k; 32 x 1k; 8 x 8k; 128 x 2k; 256 x 1k.

**Why 120k, not 128k.** The first attempt (job 1448) crashed in its smoke test with a device-side assert on the
first step whose tokens sat past position 131,071. vLLM builds Llama-3.1's rotary-embedding table with
`max_position_embeddings` = 131,072 entries (`rotary_embedding/base.py`, `_compute_cos_sin_cache`), and
`VLLM_ALLOW_LONG_MAX_MODEL_LEN` does not enlarge it. So Llama-3.1-8B cannot serve contexts above 131,072 tokens
in vLLM, and the longest census context is 120k plus an 8k chunk. (This also limits Stage 3: TraceLab sessions
past 128k need a cap or another model.) The kernel job was cancelled after 103 shapes so that both jobs measure
the same grid; its 128k rows are kept in `results/microbench/attn_f7_128k_partial.jsonl` (960 rows, 0 errors).

## Results

All numbers come from `reports/regimes_study/figures/census_tables.md` (generated by `analysis/regime_census.py`
from job 1452's telemetry and job 1453's `attn_f7.jsonl`) unless a line says otherwise. "Loss" = default-config
step time / the fastest alternative that does the same work. "eff" = roofline / measured, where the roofline is
the H100 spec peak (3.35 TB/s, 989 TFLOPS). Those peaks are not reachable in practice, so eff is an upper bound on
the room left, not a target.

### Summary

Only two kinds of step lose much against an alternative that does the same work, and both involve a long decode
sharing an attention launch with a prefill:

- **X steps** (a prefill plus a long decode in a step over 512 tokens): median loss 1.57x over 43 shapes, up to 3.36x.
  The step runs without CUDA graphs, so vLLM leaves FA3's split count to FA3, which gives the whole launch 1 split.
  One 120k decode next to a short-prefix prefill then takes 1.45 ms of attention per layer instead of 0.19-0.26 ms. Capture 2048 recovers this up to 2,048
  tokens. Above that (loss up to 1.78x), only a split bound in eager steps or separate launches would.
- **Long-prefix appends next to a long decode (A+L, new label):** up to 1.48x, even in steps of <= 512 tokens with
  split bound 32. FA3 sizes splits for the whole launch, and an expensive prefill already fills the GPU, so the decode
  still gets 1 split (kernel notes: `dyn_splits max=1`). Launching decodes and prefill separately recovers it.

Two batch-shape problems sit on top:
- **H5 wave edges:** 1.14-1.53x in attention for the same tokens.
- **The token budget (H3):** an 8,192-token step costs 211 ms fresh and 1,110 ms on a 120k prefix, and running
  decodes wait the whole time.

Decode-only steps, ordinary prefill steps and mixed steps are within about 5% of every alternative measured.
Eager launch cost, which my prediction expected to matter, is a median 0.3 ms at this node's load.

### Data quality

- 2,175 probe and decode steps per config; none rejected for a shape mismatch (every probe step had exactly the
  designed q, cached prefix and decode set).
- Repeats: median spread (max/min - 1) 1.9%, and 81% of shapes are within 5%. The gate (all within 5%) is **not fully
  met**. The misses are random: each repeat's mean deviation from the median is 0.2-0.6%, and the 90th percentile is
  <= 2.9%. They are concentrated in short steps: under 15 ms, 52% are within 5%; at 40 ms and above, 92-95%. The
  node's load average was 86-173 during the run. Differences under 10% on steps under 15 ms are not used below.
- Kernel census: 420 shapes x 9-10 configs, 4,096 rows, 0 errors.
- The kernel harness compares its split-bound-32 metadata with vLLM's real graph builder. They differ in 139 of 192
  rows of <= 512 tokens; Stage 1's sweep matched them all. The mismatched rows still behave like split bound 32 in the
  engine. Fit step time minus 32 x kernel attention as a line in T on the 38 matched in-engine rows; the 124
  mismatched rows sit a median +0.7 ms from it. Had vLLM run FA3's own choice on them, they would sit -5 to -42 ms
  away (computed in the session from the same files, not in the script). So the difference does not change the split
  path, but its cause is not identified.

### The 512-token boundary has three separate causes

Steps one token apart (T = 512 vs 513), default config (from `census_priced.jsonl`):

| What is in the step | Jump at 513 | With capture 2048 | Cause |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1-3 decodes of 120k (with or without 31 x 1k) + a prefill on prefix 0-8k | 2.24-3.59x | gone (1.05-1.08x) | the decode loses split bound 32 (H1) |
| a prefill alone | 1.21x (prefix 0) to 1.61x (120k) | stays: 1.11x (prefix 0) to 1.66x (120k) | wave edge (H5): one chunk of 512 tokens fills 128 of 132 SMs (8 KV heads x 16 tiles of 32 tokens); 513 needs a second wave |
| 8-256 decodes of 1k-8k + a prefill | 1.00-1.19x | mostly stays | wave edge plus a small graph-mode difference |

The capture size and the wave edge are both 512 for this model. Removing CUDA graphs' 512 limit alone does not
remove the prefill-alone jump. **Eager launch cost** (default minus capture 2048 for steps of 513-2,048 tokens, net
of the attention change) is a median 0.3 ms over 128 shapes. Capture 2048 makes those steps a median 1.00x faster,
except X steps. At this load, CPU launch time is hidden under GPU time (the median step spends 0.22 ms of CPU per ms
of GPU, computed in the session from the telemetry).

### H5 against the revised criterion

Revised support clause: an alternative that processes the same tokens is >= 1.15x faster in total attention time,
measured. Kernel level, the aligned chunk now plus the remainder in a later step of <= 512 tokens:

| Prefix | 513 tokens (512 + 16) | 544 (512 + 32) | 1,057 (1,056 + 16) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 8k | 1.40x | 1.38x | 1.14x |
| 32k | 1.53x | 1.51x | 1.19x |
| 120k | 1.46x | 1.46x | 1.23x |

SM efficiency (tile model) is 52-53% at 513-544 tokens and 69-72% at 1,057. **8 of 9 shapes meet the kernel-level
clause; the miss is 1,057 tokens on 8k (1.14x).** The other clause (trace-weighted efficiency < 80%) needs Stage 3
traces, so H5 has no verdict yet.

Whole step, in-engine:
- **Remainder in its own step** (pays a second step's fixed cost), 513-544 tokens: 1.27-1.28x at 120k (115.2 -> 90.2
  ms at 513), 1.06-1.08x at 32k, and a loss at 8k (23.5 -> 26.5 ms). At 1,057 tokens: 1.10x at 120k, none below.
- **Remainder riding in a step that runs anyway** (partly modelled: the remainder's attention from the kernel census
  plus 0.020 ms per token of other kernels), 513-544 tokens: 1.45-1.48x at 120k, 1.36-1.39x at 32k, 1.27-1.30x at 8k.

So the fix pays only where the scheduler can attach the remainder to other work, or where the prefix is long.

Filling the idle wave with other work, kernel level (two appends, one launch vs two):

| Pair | 32k prefix | 120k prefix | Tiles |
|---|---|---|---|
| 544 + 512 | 1.32x faster together | 1.33x | 264 = exactly 2 waves |
| 513 + 513 | 1.31x | 1.27x | 272 |
| 512 + 512 | 0.99x | 1.06x | 256 |
| 1,056 + 32 | **0.83x (together is slower)** | **0.80x** | 272 |

The last row is the mirror-image hazard. A 1,056-token chunk exactly fills two waves, and adding a 32-token chunk to
the same launch costs a third wave (1.2-1.25x). A wave-aware chunker has to avoid tipping an aligned launch over the
edge as well as align single chunks.

### H3 at the step level

The same 8,192-token budget at different cached prefixes, default config, prefill alone: 211.5 ms (prefix 0),
271.1 ms (8k), 445.7 ms (32k), 1,109.9 ms (120k). With 1 x 120k or 32 x 1k decodes in the step it takes the same at
the 120k prefix (1,107 and 1,105 ms). Those decodes normally step in 11.7 ms and 8.2 ms, so one 8k chunk on a 120k
prefix costs each of them 95-135 decode steps' worth of time. The token budget hides a 5.2x range in step
cost. Whether this matters depends on how often such chunks meet running decodes (Stages 2-3).

### Qualitative predictions, checked

1. **Decode-only steps sit near the bandwidth floor and the attention kernel moves them <= 5%:** confirmed. The best
   kernel moves the step <= 1.9% (median 0.4%); eff 69-85%. Long-context decode at kernel level: FA3 with bound 32 is
   within 1% of the best configuration for 1-16 sequences at 64k and 120k.
2. **Steps above the capture size pay an eager launch cost that capture 2048 removes:** not supported at this load.
   Eager excess median 0.3 ms; capture 2048 helps only X steps, through the split bound.
3. **X steps lose to both the split choice and the eager cliff; capture 2048 fixes both:** partly right. The loss is
   the split choice (eager excess 1% of the step). Capture 2048 reaches the best alternative within 5% for 24 of 31 X
   shapes up to 2,048 tokens. It cannot reach the 12 shapes above 2,048 tokens (loss up to 1.78x).
4. **Long-prefix appends keep the H5 loss under every engine config; a remainder of <= 64 tokens on 120k in a step of
   <= 512 tokens costs <= 0.25 ms of attention per layer:** confirmed. Capture 2048 leaves the prefill-alone jump in
   place (115.2 ms default vs 118.7 ms capture 2048 at 513 tokens on 120k). The remainder costs 0.186 / 0.187 / 0.251
   ms per layer at 16 / 32 / 64 tokens; 64 tokens sits at the bound.

Not predicted: the A+L straggler in graph-sized steps; the coincidence of the 512 capture size with the wave edge;
the mirror-image hazard (1,056 + 32).

## Step-level evidence for H1-H5

These are not verdicts. The kill and support criteria are shares of production GPU time, which come from Stages 2-3.

| Hyp. | Census evidence |
|---|---|
| H1 | Loss up to 3.36x (513-2,048 tokens) and 1.78x (> 2,048). At a 120k prefix the straggler still costs up to 1.48x (A+L), but only separate launches recover it; a split bound does not. |
| H2 | Launch cost ~0 at load average 86-173 (median 0.3 ms). The 512 boundary costs through the split bound (H1) and the wave edge (H5), not through launches. |
| H3 | One 8,192-token step: 211 ms (fresh) to 1,110 ms (120k prefix); co-scheduled decodes wait the whole step. |
| H4 | X loss is largest for short-prefix chunks next to 120k decodes: 1.6-3.4x for 512-1,057-token chunks at prefix 0-8k (1.1-1.8x for 2-4k chunks), 1.1-1.9x at 32k. |
| H5 | Kernel-level clause met (1.14-1.53x, 8 of 9 shapes >= 1.15x); whole step 1.28x at 120k even with the remainder in its own step. |

## Caveats

- **Designed grid, not traffic.** Each shape counts once. Which regimes matter depends on how often production
  forms them (Stages 2-3).
- **Swapped-in alternatives.** The split, separate-launch and other-kernel alternatives replace the measured
  attention (1 layer, x 32) in the in-engine step; they were not run in the engine. Capture 2048 and the H5
  remainder steps were measured in the engine.
- **One node under shared load.** The eager cost depends on CPU load: at a load average of ~650 the eager floor was
  16.8 ms (job 1040), at 86-173 it is hidden. A busier host would bring H2 back.
- **Metadata mismatch** in the kernel harness for 139 rows (see Data quality); in-engine times are consistent with
  split bound 32.
- **Contexts stop at 120k** (Llama-3.1's RoPE table in vLLM).
- **eff is relative to spec peaks**; prefill steps at 45-60% are typical of FA3 and cuBLAS, so most of that gap is
  not recoverable by batch formation.
